# Conversion and microcopy

## Behaviour model
**Fogg Behavior Model, B = MAP.** A behaviour happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt meet at the same moment. If a conversion fails, one of the three is missing. Wireframe check: is the value clear (motivation), is the action easy (ability), is there a visible trigger at the right moment (prompt)?

## Friction (Baymard, checkout research)
- Global average cart abandonment tracked at about 70%.
- An ideal checkout can be as short as 12 form elements (7 fields). The average US checkout shows about 23.
- Baymard estimates large e-commerce sites can gain about 35% conversion from checkout design alone.
- Rules: cut fields, single column forms, inline validation, keep entered data after errors, guest checkout, mark optional fields.

## Button copy
- ContentVerve / Unbounce test: "Start my free 30-day trial" beat "Start your free 30-day trial" by about 90% in click-through. A second test saw about 25% fewer clicks with "your".
- Treat as a strong test idea, not a rule. Effect size depends on audience.
- Name the outcome, not the action: "Get my quote" over "Submit".
- Say "free" when it is free.
- Put doubt removers next to the button (cancel anytime, no card needed). Test them: one Unbounce test saw a privacy line lower sign ups.

## Placement and proof
- Primary CTA in the first screen and repeated after proof blocks.
- Social proof near the decision point: logos, numbers, short quotes with names.
- One accent color reserved for the primary action (Von Restorff).
- Contrast with the page matters more than the button color itself.

## Ethics line
No fake scarcity, no confirm shaming, no pre ticked add-ons. These lift short term numbers and lose trust. The skill flags them.
